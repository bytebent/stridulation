## Mechanism

| Source | What stridulation takes from it |
|---|---|
| Bennet-Clark & Bailey 2002, *Ticking of the clockwork cricket: the role of the escapement mechanism*, J. Exp. Biol. 205:613–625 | The escapement phase model: plectrum strikes a tooth at the low point of the file's swing (270°), drives it through the first part of the cycle, is released as the file swings up; impact and release both sound. The escapement's tick sits at about 2× the carrier when impact and release are 180° apart, irregular when not. A cricket escapement starts on its own and runs while each impulse exceeds the losses. Faults: jumped teeth, noisy catch then hold, free exponential ring-down when the escapement stops (Q 23). First pulses of a chirp start mid-file and use less file. Table 1 carriers and within-pulse falls: *G. campestris* 5 kHz, 10%; *G. bimaculatus* 4.7 kHz, 10.6%; *Modicogryllus* 5 kHz, 16%; *Acheta* 4.5 kHz, 13%; *Nemobius* 4 kHz, 10%; *Eugryllodes* 3.2 kHz, 4.7%; *Oecanthus pellucens* 3 kHz, 13%; *G. gryllotalpa* 1.7 kHz, under 6%; *G. vineae* 3.4 kHz, 6%; *Scapteriscus* 2.7 kHz, 7.5%; *Rufocephalus* 3.2 kHz, 0–1.6% (calls from a sharply tuned burrow); *Tympanophora similis* (katydid) 6.5 kHz, 7.7%. Basis for Contact, Tick, Glide, Arc growth. |
| Bennet-Clark 2003, *Wing resonances in the Australian field cricket Teleogryllus oceanicus*, J. Exp. Biol. 206:1479–1496, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.00281) | Right file 252 teeth, closer at the posterior end met first. Free resonances: left wing 4.56 kHz (Q 25.1), right 4.21 kHz (Q 23.9); song 4.8 kHz. Right wing driven on the file 5.88 kHz, via the left plectrum 4.83 kHz: the plectrum adds stiffness. Damped pulses from pushing a plectrum across the file 4.82 kHz, Q 23.4. Resonant mass about 1.4 mg (file, anal area and harp), not the 0.22 mg harp alone. Glide partly from the flexible anterior file region, where the driven resonance is lower; vibration amplitude rises from the posterior end to the middle then falls. Muscle force rises then falls; closely spaced early teeth let the wing accelerate. Basis for two wings, Wings, Glide by position, Curve. |
| Koch, Elliott, Schäffner & Kleindienst 1988, *The mechanics of stridulation of the cricket Gryllus campestris*, J. Comp. Physiol. A 162:213–223, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00606086) | Plectrum speed proportional to tooth spacing, slope 4.12 kHz against a 4.2 kHz song; CV under 8% (mean 4.9%) at a given file position. Occasional exactly 2× (once 3×) closing speed, never in between. Closing speed, tooth rate and carrier Q₁₀ about 0.07 (0.7%/°C) from 20–30 °C, while opening speed and duration change strongly. Harps removed: closing up to 3× faster, CV 22%, 10–18 × 10³ teeth/s, sound 3.8–6.5 kHz plus a 9 kHz peak and rising from 4 to over 8 kHz within a stroke; toothless file about 20 × 10³/s. Cog rattle vs clockwork models, and the stroke starting as a cog rattle that tunes into clockwork. Harp Q 25 (Nocke 1971, cited). Basis for the force-driven stroke and tests 1–3. |
| Sismondo 1993, *Ultrasubharmonic resonance and nonlinear dynamics in the song of Oecanthus nigricornis*, Int. J. Insect Morphol. Embryol. 22:217–231 | Song 25–65 pulses/s, carrier 3.0–4.6 kHz, both temperature dependent. Carrier = tooth rate in normal song; rarely n:m locks (4:3, 5:4, about 6:5) and beats between the two tegmina. Principal mode 3.8–4.2 kHz, Q 25–35, log decrement 0.09–0.125. Carrier rises smoothly 3.0 kHz at 15 °C to 4.6 kHz at 32 °C with no jump at the resonance: tooth rate drives the wing (no escapement); missing teeth speed the plectrum only 10–15%. One file had 43 teeth, amplitude rising over 30–32 cycles. Model: frame oscillator with a 5th-power stiffening spring coupled to a linear output oscillator, 25 µs equal impulses, RK4; ±5% perturbation stays natural. Basis for the wing model, Nonlinear, the tree cricket and test 5. |
| Lindeman & Yack 2019, *Bark beetles use a spring-loaded mechanism to produce variable song patterns*, J. Exp. Biol. 222:jeb190660, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1242/jeb.190660) | *Dendroctonus valens*: about 63 teeth, pulse-to-tooth about 1:1, about 695 pulses/s. Simple chirps 13.6 ± 1.7 ms, 9.1 ± 1.1 pulses; interrupted 54 ± 13.1 ms, 15.8 ± 3.8 pulses, about 3% of chirps; plectrum catches and deflects 8–12.5 ms before release; travel about 213 µm on a 638 µm file; spectral peaks about 7 and 30 kHz. Interruptions are several catch-and-release events. Basis for Spring and test 7. |

## Radiators and groups

| Source | What stridulation takes from it |
|---|---|
| Masters, Tautz, Fletcher & Markl 1983, *Body vibration and sound production in an insect (Atta sexdens) without specialized radiating structures*, J. Comp. Physiol. 150:239–249, [doi](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00606374) | File teeth about 2 µm high, 3 µm apart. Each strike rings the gaster at about 1.05 kHz (period 0.94 ms, dipole swing) and about 10 kHz (Q 1–3, often heavier); broadband to 30–40 kHz, rarely 60. Strike interval 1.7 ± 0.4 ms, avoiding 1 ms; double strikes about 0.5 ms apart; no correlation with the body period. Strokes about 50 ms, about five per second. Underwater the modes drop 15% and 35%. Basis for Body and the ant preset. |
| Elsner 1974, *Neuroethology of sound production in gomphocerine grasshoppers I*, J. Comp. Physiol. 88:67–102 | Terms and *C. biguttulus* timing: impulse 0.9 ms, syllable 9.1 ms, chirp 72.5 ms, first-order sequence 2.9 s, second-order 15 s. Leg paths step-like or vibrating. Left and right legs play different patterns, rarely in sync, phase lag timed to the millisecond fills the gaps; legs swap roles via proprioceptive feedback; one leg alone plays one pattern only. *O. viridulus*: impulse rate 0.2–0.5 per ms mid-upstroke, 3 at its end, 4 on the downstroke; upstroke 54 ms (pattern I) vs 60 ms (II); courtship sequences 40–50 s with 10–15 s pauses. Peg impact carrier about 8 kHz in most species. Basis for two limbs, Shape and the grasshopper presets. |
